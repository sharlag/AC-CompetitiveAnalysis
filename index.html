Mission Home Remodeling High threat

SF-based design-build firm with a professionally rebranded site, 54 Yelp reviews, 548 photos, and a clear story around historic SF home restoration. Actively runs promotions ($2k off design packages) and operates Mon–Fri 8am–6pm with walk-ins welcome. Covers SF, Marin, Peninsula, and East Bay.

Hadley General Contractors High threat

Belvedere/Tiburon-based, 3rd-generation family-owned firm celebrating 75 years (founded 1950) — a longer legacy story than Ashbury's 30 years. Serves SF, Marin, Napa, and Sonoma with custom homes and remodels. Pairs "old world craftsmanship" messaging with cloud-based project management software and an explicit transparent/open-book pricing promise. Site has prominent lead capture (Schedule Call CTA plus a downloadable "7 things to do before your build" lead magnet), polished case-study portfolio (Stinson Beach, Belvedere, Golden Gate projects), and active LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, and Pinterest presence.

Ashbury SWOT vs. the competitive field
Strengths
  • 30+ years in SF — genuine longevity very few competitors match
  • Vertically integrated — self-performs most work (a real differentiator)
  • Custom woodworking shop in-house (rare)
  • Covers both SF and Marin (dual-market reach)
  • Notable commercial project (The Midway SF) — credibility signal
  • Kevin's stated responsiveness is a direct competitive advantage
Weaknesses
  • No design-build positioning — a story competitors are telling well
  • No clear CTA or lead capture on the site
  • Review presence thin — only Houzz, very few platforms
  • Site aesthetics undercut the premium work being done
  • Full remodels aren't featured despite being the primary goal
  • No green/sustainability credentials visible
Opportunities
  • Seismic/foundation work is a niche few competitors own clearly
  • SF Victorian and Edwardian restoration is an underleveraged story
  • Kevin's responsiveness can be a marketed differentiator ("we call back")
  • Yelp, Google Reviews, and Houzz expansion is low-hanging fruit
  • The woodworking shop is a visual/story asset almost no competitor has
  • Architect and designer referral network (currently untapped on site)
Threats
  • Mission Home Remodeling has made a significant brand investment and is actively scaling
  • Hadley General Contractors out-tenures Ashbury (75 yrs vs. 30) and backs that story with active lead capture and a case-study portfolio Ashbury's site doesn't match
  • Perez Construction is building a strong high-end brand with designer relationships and a compelling origin story
  • Upscale Construction operates at estate scale with architect partnerships and award recognition — directly competing for the same premium SF clients
  • Design-build framing is becoming the industry default — Ashbury risks appearing dated
  • Competitors with active social feeds and large photo libraries dominate discovery
  • Green/sustainability expectations growing in SF and Marin market
Key strategic opportunities

Ashbury's clearest gap in the market: No competitor clearly owns the story of being a multi-decade SF contractor who self-performs structural, foundation, and finish work under one roof. Most competitors are design-build firms that emphasize aesthetics. Ashbury can own a different promise — the contractor who handles what others won't — combining foundation-to-finish credibility with the custom cabinetry shop as proof of craft at both ends of the project.

Quickest competitive move: Get on Yelp with photos and a handful of verified reviews. Mission Home Remodeling has 548 photos and 54 reviews on Yelp alone. That is where SF homeowners are actively searching, and right now Ashbury is essentially invisible there.
